# The Molasses Flood (Boston, 15 Jan 1919) — DRAFT

## Facts (verify before final; sources: Boston Public Library / Smithsonian / Stephen Puleo, "Dark Tide")
- Purity Distilling Co. tank, North End: ~50 ft tall, ~90 ft wide, ~2.3 million US gal of molasses.
- Built fast, leaked from day one; painted brown so the leaks were less visible. Never properly tested.
- 15 Jan 1919: a fresh warm load pushed in on top of cold molasses; temperature rose to ~40 F after days near 0 F.
- Wave ~25 ft high, ~35 mph. 21 killed, ~150 injured. Harbour ran brown for months.
- Lawsuit: one of the first big class actions in Massachusetts; led to engineers' sign-off on building plans.

## Script (~200 words, ~55 s)
Boston, 1919. A wave of molasses, twenty-five feet tall, chased people down the street.
It was a tank the size of a small block, holding two million gallons of molasses.
The company knew it leaked. So they painted it brown.
Nobody tested it. Nobody checked it. They just filled it up.
Then a warm day arrived, and a fresh warm load went in on top of the cold syrup.
The steel groaned. Rivets popped. And the whole tank let go.
The wave hit at thirty-five miles an hour. Faster than anyone could run.
Luckily, it smelled lovely. Unluckily, that is not why it killed twenty-one people.
Think of honey in the fridge. Cold, it creeps. Warm, it pours. And a whole tank of it pushes with huge force on every wall.
The harbour ran brown for months. The smell stayed for years.
The court case changed the rules: from then on, an engineer had to sign the plans.
So next time something is painted to hide a problem, ask what is behind the paint.
